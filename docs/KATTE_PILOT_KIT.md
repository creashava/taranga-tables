# Katte Pilot Kit v2.0: Operational Manual

Prepared for the **Taranga** team in Mysuru.  
Status: **PROVISIONAL / PILOT-READY**.  
*Non-clinical, non-legal: Have legal counsel check consent/DPDP matters, a Chartered Accountant review deposits/accounting, and a psychology faculty advisor review wellbeing instruments.*

---

## 0. The Pilot at a Glance

* **Goal**: Learn whether strangers come back for a second table, and whether the Katte rituals make people want to. Built to find out what is true, not to prove the idea.
* **Scale**: Exactly **2 Kattes of 6 people = 12 places**. Recruit 20–24 sign-ups to choose well and keep a waitlist.
* **Length**: ~4 weeks (letters in Week 1, then three tables about a week apart).
* **Audience**: Adults (18+) who live or study in Mysuru. Friends and family of the team are excluded from measurement.
* **Hosts**: One lead host and one backup host from Taranga at every table.
* **Place**: A public cafe with a quiet corner or room for 90 minutes.
* **Cost to Participants**: **Free** (no upfront deposit in this first pilot).

### 0.1 What is Different from the First Kit
1. **Cultural Identity**: A pod is now a **Katte** (the raised stone/earthen platform under a banyan tree where neighbours sit, talk, grumble, and play games). Made radically inclusive: open to all genders and backgrounds; every ritual and card is optional.
2. **Opener Experiment**:
   * **Katte A**: Opens with the **Peeve Round** (12 situational peeves + Pivot question: *"What would the perfect version look like?"*).
   * **Katte B**: Opens with **Favourites Cards** (12 favourite memories/places).
3. **The Relay**: Katte A and Katte B write 140-character relay slips at Table 2 and exchange them at Table 3.
4. **The Elder's Game**: Traditional games (Chowka Bhara, Aadu Huli Aata, Alagulimane, Lagori) at Table 2.
5. **The Banyan Tree**: Shared offline hours tracked on an A3 banyan sheet with leaf stickers.

## 1. The Katte Rules & Host Principles

### 1.1 The 5 Katte Rules (Print on a Card & Read Aloud at Table 1)
1. **Everyone gets a seat.**
2. **Grumble gently**: about things and situations, never about people.
3. **Newcomers and elders both talk, and both listen.**
4. **What is said on the katte stays on the katte.**
5. **Anyone can walk away, and nobody walks away alone.**

### 1.2 Inclusion Promise (Say Aloud)
> *"This katte is for everyone, whatever your gender, background, language or age. Every game and card is optional. If something doesn't work for you, tell us and we will change it."*

### 1.3 What the Host Never Does
* Shames anyone.
* Forces a card, question, or game.
* Shares what members said outside the Katte.
* Sells anything or pitches services.
* Photographs without unanimous explicit opt-in consent (default is **no photos**).
* Leaves anyone alone with a problem.

## 2. Table Run-Sheets (90 Minutes Each)

### Table 1: Take Your Seat
* `0:00–0:10` **Take Your Seat**: Greet by first name, hand out name cards, assist with ordering, soft background music. Let people pick seats. No forced introductions.
* `0:10–0:15` **Welcome**: Read welcome script, introduce Taranga, read the 5 Katte Rules and Inclusion Promise. Confirm photos are OFF. Invite phones into the basket on silent.
* `0:15–0:30` **Opener**: Katte A runs Peeve Round + Pivot; Katte B runs Favourites. ~30 seconds per person.
* `0:30–0:45` **Plant the Banyan**: Katte selects its name (everyone writes an idea on paper; group picks) and sticks the first leaves on the Banyan sheet.
* `0:45–1:05` **Letter Box or Place Story**: Read printed Letter 3 cards (if permitted) or tell a short story of a place in Mysuru that matters.
* `1:05–1:20` **Table Cards**: Group picks a level together (Light, Real, Deep). Answer 1–2 cards. Never push deeper.
* `1:20–1:30` **Close**: One-word check-out (*"One word for how you feel"*), Table 2 details, anonymous 3-minute feedback QR, thank you, offer company to transport point.

### Table 2: The Elder's Game
* `0:00–0:10` **Take Your Seat**: One-word check-in.
* `0:10–0:20` **Learn the Game**: Taught by an invited elder or prepared host (Chowka Bhara, Aadu Huli Aata, Alagulimane, or Lagori).
* `0:20–0:55` **Play**: Mix teams, rotate seats halfway. Low stakes, zero betting/money. Skipping allowed.
* `0:55–1:05` **Reflect**: *"What did the game make you notice about how our group works together?"*
* `1:05–1:15` **Relay Lines**: Each person writes one line (up to 140 chars) on a slip to the other Katte: *"To the next Katte: One thing about us..."*
* `1:15–1:25` **Pod Project**: Katte picks one small civic improvement project (< 3 hours total) to do before Table 3.
* `1:25–1:30` **Close**: One-word check-out, optional Stretch card, feedback QR.

### Table 3: Keep the Katte
* `0:00–0:10` **Take Your Seat**: One-word check-in.
* `0:10–0:25` **Relay Swap**: Hosts swap the Relay pages from both Kattes. Members read them aloud or silently.
* `0:25–0:45` **Seen Cards**: Each member writes a kind, specific note about what each other person said or did (not looks). Sealed in envelopes for home.
* `0:45–1:00` **Pod Project Presentation**: Katte shares what they did, learned, or decided. Celebrate effort, not polish.
* `1:00–1:10` **Banyan & Passport**: Add final leaves (reaching "Young tree" stage), stamp Pod Passports.
* `1:10–1:20` **Keep the Katte**: Private slip choice (Keep meeting / Attend Strangers & Co / Part kindly) + mutual double-opt-in Contact Share slip.
* `1:20–1:30` **Close**: One-word check-out, final feedback QR, thank you, mention next Taranga community event.

## 3. Safety, Helplines & Escalation Ladder

### 3.1 Discreet Safety Signal
* **The Signal**: *"Ask a host for a refill of water."*
* **Host Action**: Treat as an immediate directive to step in, gently interrupt the conversation, and offer the member a quiet, unpressured exit or check-in.

### 3.2 Escalation Ladder
* **Level 1 (Minor Discomfort)**: A card feels too personal; member looks quiet or anxious.  
  *Action*: Adjust activity, offer a free pass, check in quietly during breaks.
* **Level 2 (Conduct Concern)**: Unkind remarks, pushing someone to share, unwanted personal questions.  
  *Action*: Private conversation, clear gentle warning, log in restricted incident file. If repeated, ask member to leave privately.
* **Level 3 (Serious Incident / Distress)**: Harassment, threats, panic attack, medical emergency.  
  *Action*: Keep person safe and never leave them alone. Call **112** if in danger. Inform Safety Lead immediately. Affected person decides whether to report to authorities; team provides complete support.

### 3.3 Emergency Helplines (Verify before printing)
* **National Emergency**: `112`
* **Tele-MANAS (Mental Health Support)**: `14416`
* **Women's Helpline**: `181`

## 4. Measurement & Decision Rubric

### 4.1 Four Pass Marks (Agree in Writing Before Table 1)
1. **Second-Meeting Return Rate**: At least 50% of members who attended Table 1 return for Table 2.
2. **Felt Safe Score**: Average safety score $\ge 4.0 / 5.0$ across tables.
3. **Zero Serious Safety Incidents**: No unresolved Level 2/3 conduct or safety concerns.
4. **Qualitative Recall**: Members unpromptedly mention key rituals (Relay, Elder's Game, Banyan, Seen Cards) in feedback.

### 4.2 Post-Pilot Decision Matrix
* **All 4 Pass Marks Met**: Run Pilot 2 with 2–4 new Kattes; test a refundable deposit arm; begin digital app features supporting the rituals people valued most.
* **Safe, but < 50% Return**: Change one structural variable (cadence, venue, time of day) and rerun the pilot before building software.
* **Unresolved Safety Concern**: Full stop. Review incident logs and do not restart until protocols are fortified.
* **Loved Only Because of Hosts**: Pivot towards building a host-led community rather than a standalone app.
